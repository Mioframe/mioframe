## Identity and evolution

Buttons initiate a discrete action. Material defines default action buttons and, in M3 Expressive, toggle buttons for binary selection. Buttons may have an optional leading icon. Five color configurations establish emphasis: elevated, filled, tonal, outlined, and text. Five sizes are available: extra small, small (the default), medium, large, and extra large. Shapes are round (the default) or square.

The May 2025 Expressive update added the expanded size and shape ranges, toggle behavior, pressed and selected shape morphing, and a recommended 16dp horizontal padding for small buttons; the earlier 24dp small padding remains available but is no longer recommended. Compared with M2, M3 uses dynamic-color mappings, gives icons and labels the same color, no longer recommends neutral text buttons, standardizes leading/trailing icons at 20dp, and changes the default from a 36dp slightly rounded container to a 40dp fully rounded one.

Platform status in the source is available for the design kit, Flutter, Jetpack Compose, Jetpack Compose Expressive, Android Views, Android Views Expressive, and Web. Web Expressive is listed as unavailable.

## Anatomy and content

The anatomy is label text, container, and an optional icon. The label is the primary element and names the resulting action. It should ideally contain one to three words, use sentence case, remain fully visible, and not truncate or wrap in ordinary presentation. A container grows with its content; a fixed width must not be narrower than the label. Round containers use fully rounded corners. Square-container corner size varies by button size. Text-button containers are visually absent until hover, focus, or press.

At most one icon accompanies the label. It precedes the label in reading order: left in left-to-right content and right in right-to-left content. It must communicate the action clearly and must not be stacked vertically with the label. Toggle buttons should use an outlined icon when unselected and a filled icon when selected; when no filled icon exists, increased icon weight is the alternative.

Outlined buttons use a 1dp outline at every size. Extra-small and small buttons require a target area of at least 48×48dp. Label typography is label large for extra-small and small, title medium for medium, headline small for large, and headline large for extra large, with the exact component-token aliases and resolved values recorded below.

Pressing morphs either starting shape to the size-specific pressed corner. Toggle selection swaps the shape family: round becomes the documented square corner and square becomes full. Shape restoration follows state exit. The size token sets assign the fast spatial spring with damping `0.6` and stiffness `800` to corner-size motion. The accessibility page additionally recommends a modified motion curve on the web when rapid successive activation could create resonant overlapping animations.

## States and behavior

Default buttons perform an action. Toggle buttons maintain a binary unselected/selected state and may change color, shape, icon, and label. The default selected morph changes round to square; the inverse shape mapping is specified for initially square toggles. If labels differ between selection states, their character counts should remain similar to prevent disruptive layout changes.

The specs document enabled/default, disabled, hovered, focused, pressed, selected, and unselected presentations across the applicable color configurations. Hover, focus, and press use state-layer colors and opacities; focus also uses the focus indicator; elevation varies by color and state. Disabled tokens reduce container, label, icon, and outline visibility as applicable. Exact state colors, opacities, elevations, outlines, and selected/unselected mappings are in the catalogue below. The deprecated baseline token sets additionally retain dragged-state tokens where published; the current Expressive state diagrams do not separately prescribe dragged button behavior.

## Usage guidance

- Use buttons for discrete actions and keep their number low enough to preserve hierarchy. Put lower-priority actions in an overflow menu, navigation rail, chips, text links, or icon buttons when those components better fit the action.
- Filled has the greatest button emphasis after a FAB and is for important flow-completing actions; use it sparingly, ideally once per page. Tertiary filled color is allowed in some contexts.
- Tonal provides less emphasis than filled and more than outlined, using secondary color mapping. Outlined represents medium-emphasis supporting actions and pairs with filled; keep it on simple backgrounds or provide sufficient contrasting fill over imagery. Elevated is tonal plus shadow and is reserved for separation from a visually prominent background.
- Text is the lowest-priority style and is common in cards, dialogs, and snackbars. Its default container is not visible, so its label must remain distinguishable from ordinary text. Do not underline it as a hyperlink substitute. Dialog text buttons align to the trailing edge.
- Do not confuse outlined or text buttons with visually similar chips, text, or links. Prefer filled or tonal when distinction is weak.
- Width may fit content or respond to the layout, but avoid excessively long, flat buttons on large screens. Keep icon and label grouped and centered as width changes.
- Placement, size, and arrangement may adapt across window sizes, but preserve source order so screen-reader and keyboard navigation remain consistent.

## Accessibility and input

Buttons must be operable and navigable with assistive technology. `Tab` moves focus to a button; `Space` or `Enter` activates it. The accessible label should match the visible label and may add necessary context. Enabled elevated, filled, and tonal containers need at least 3:1 contrast with the background; for outlined and text buttons, measure that ratio from label text to background.

Keep labels concise under text scaling. On Android at 200% text size, labels should fit within two lines; if truncation still occurs, provide a one-tap alternative that exposes the full content. This platform allowance does not replace the ordinary guidance to keep labels on one line. Extra-small and small visual containers retain a minimum 48×48dp interactive target.

## Related official contracts

- Button groups define coordinated collections of buttons and icon buttons.
- Icon buttons are the alternative for compact icon-only actions.
- FABs outrank filled buttons in visual impact and represent the highest-emphasis primary action.
- Cards, dialogs, and snackbars commonly compose text buttons; their contracts own the surrounding layout.
- Hyperlinked body text, chips, menus, and navigation components are alternatives where the content is navigation, selection, overflow, or persistent structure rather than a discrete labeled action.
- Material state layers, focus indicators, dynamic color, type scale, shape scale, elevation, and motion springs supply the aliases referenced by the component tokens.
